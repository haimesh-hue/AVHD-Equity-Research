AppLovin Corporation APP

An AI ad-targeting engine that buys and sells ads inside mobile games, now trying to do the same for web shops. Exceptional economics, a share price down about 60% in 2026, and a story that rests on the next two quarters of growth.

1Business overview What does it do and how did it get here?

How it makes money

Advertisers (mostly mobile game studios, increasingly e-commerce brands) set a return-on-ad-spend target in Axon Ads Manager. AppLovin's AI engine, Axon, bids for ad slots across thousands of apps that use its MAX mediation auction, and keeps a cut of every winning ad. Revenue is booked as the platform's take, so gross margin is about 88%.

Since the Apps (own games) business was sold to Tripledot on 30 Jun 2025, AppLovin is a pure advertising company with fewer than 900 employees.

Timeline

A software marketplace with one product that matters: the Axon model. Revenue per employee is above $7M, which tells you how little labour the machine needs.

2Moat Why does it earn its returns, and can rivals copy it?

Real advantages

  • Data loop. MAX runs the ad auction inside a large share of mobile games, so Axon sees bids, installs and in-app purchases at a scale few rivals can match. Better predictions win more auctions, which brings more data.
  • Two-sided network. Publishers use MAX because it pays most; advertisers come because the supply is there.
  • Measured on outcomes. Advertisers buy a ROAS target, so switching is easy only if a rival proves better returns.

Where it is weaker

  • Model gains come in steps; Q2 2026 growth slowed when an update (the gen-AI video tool) slipped.
  • E-commerce is a new arena where Meta and Google own the incumbent data and creative tools.
  • Dependence on Apple and Google app-store and privacy rules; a policy change can blunt targeting.

The moat is real in mobile gaming, visible in 80%+ EBITDA margins that competitors with similar products (Unity) cannot get near. It is unproven outside games, and that is where the valuation debate sits.

3Drivers What is revenue made of?

Revenue ≈ advertiser spend through Axon × AppLovin's take rate. Spend grows with more advertisers (volume), better ROAS that lets each advertiser spend more (price), and a shift into e-commerce and web advertisers (mix).

5Ratios How efficient and how leveraged?

DuPont uses year-end assets and equity, so it differs from averaged figures on data sites (stockanalysis shows TTM ROE 204%, ROIC 133%). ROIC = EBIT × (1 − 21%) ÷ (equity + debt − cash). Receivable days look long likely because receivables are billed on gross ad spend while revenue is recorded as AppLovin's net take.

Net debt is close to zero (0.1x EBITDA), so leverage is not the issue. ROE above 100% is driven by margins, not borrowing, but book equity is small because buybacks shrink it, which flatters the ratio. ROIC near 100% says each dollar of capital earns roughly a dollar a year.

7Industry and competitors How does it stack up?

Digital advertising grows with the online economy, roughly in the high single digits to low teens a year; the AI ad engines (Meta, Google, AppLovin) have taken share from intermediaries like The Trade Desk. AppLovin's 10-K names Meta, Google, Amazon and Unity as main competitors.

stockanalysis.com, prices 20 to 21 Oct 2026. Growth = analysts' 3-year revenue CAGR forecast. Alphabet's trailing P/E is lowered by large investment gains in net income.

AppLovin has the highest margins, returns and forecast growth in the group, yet trades at about Meta's EV/EBITDA and a lower forward P/E. The market prices it as if its margins will fade toward peers or its growth is about to slow sharply.

8Management Who runs it and how do they allocate capital?

People

  • Adam Foroughi, co-founder, CEO since Dec 2011 (age 45). Product-driven; led the Apps exit and the e-commerce push.
  • Matthew Stumpf, CFO since Jan 2024, promoted from FP&A.
  • CTO Vasily Shikin moved to a non-executive engineering role (Jul 2026); legal chief Victoria Valenzuela retired as executive (Aug 2026).

Ownership and allocation

  • Insiders own about 13% to 20% of shares depending on source; 30.2M high-vote Class B shares give insiders outsized control.
  • Foroughi sold about $51M of stock at ~$480 to $500 in June 2026.
  • Capital priority is buybacks: about $8.1B spent on repurchases from 2022 through H1 2026, no acquisitions since 2022, debt held flat at ~$3.5B.

A founder who has been right on the big calls (Axon, selling the games) and a disciplined buyback machine. The weaknesses are a thin bench after two senior exits in 2026 and a governance structure outside shareholders cannot change.

9Risks What would break the story?

  • Growth deceleration. Two ~20% earnings-day drops in 2026 (Feb, Aug); analysts have cut 2027 EPS (from ~$21.2 to ~$20.0 in 60 days per 24/7 Wall St.).
  • E-commerce fails to scale. Management calls creative tools the biggest hurdle; without this leg, growth reverts to the gaming ad market.
  • Legal and regulatory. Securities class action (Talbot v. AppLovin, N.D. Cal.) alleges overstated AI capabilities; San Diego County suit alleges targeting of children and data collection without consent. The company disputes both. A reported SEC inquiry closed with no action.
  • Platform rule changes by Apple or Google on tracking or in-app ads.
  • Short-seller allegations (Fuzzy Panda 2025, CapitalWatch 2026, partly retracted) keep sentiment fragile; beta about 2.5.
  • Concentration in one model and one end market (mobile games).

The legal cases are headline risk unless they lead to restrictions on data use. The real risk is the first one: if growth falls to 20% quickly, even 16x EBITDA is not obviously cheap.

11Takeaways What to remember, what to check next

Remember

  • One of the most profitable businesses listed anywhere: 84% EBITDA margin, ~66% FCF margin, ~100% ROIC.
  • Price has fallen from ~$738 high to ~$272; valuation is now near Meta's on EV/EBITDA with faster growth.
  • The debate is durability of growth, not quality of the business.

Check next

  • Q3 2026 results (early November): revenue vs $2,055 to $2,085M guide, Q4 guide.
  • Any disclosed e-commerce advertiser count or spend.
  • Progress in the class action and San Diego suit.
  • Whether buybacks accelerate at these lower prices.

·Glossary

Adj. EBITDA
Operating profit before depreciation, amortization and stock pay; AppLovin's headline profit metric.
FCF conversion
Free cash flow ÷ net income. Above 100% means profit turns fully into cash.
ROAS
Return on ad spend: revenue an advertiser earns per dollar of ads.
Mediation (MAX)
The auction inside an app that decides which ad network fills each slot.
PEG
P/E ÷ expected EPS growth (%). Below 1 is usually read as cheap for the growth.
DuPont ROE
Net margin × asset turnover × equity multiplier (assets ÷ equity).
