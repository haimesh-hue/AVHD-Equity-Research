SNPS — Institutional Regression & Alpha Analysis

CAPM excess-return regressions vs S&P 500 Total Return · sample 1992-03 – 2026-09 · generated 2026-11-16 07:46 · QC: 0 fail, 2 warn

1. Executive summary

Conclusion (historical evidence only). No CAPM window shows statistically significant positive alpha for SNPS at the 5% level using Newey-West HAC inference. The historical record does not reject zero risk-adjusted outperformance. Over the maximum sample, CAPM (FF Mkt−RF) α = 4.72%/yr (HAC p = 0.2914); FF3 α = 5.83%/yr (HAC p = 0.1996); Carhart 4 α = 7.09%/yr (HAC p = 0.1034); FF5 α = 5.20%/yr (HAC p = 0.2654). Rolling windows with significant alpha (either sign): 6% of 36-month and 16% of 60-month windows.

Analyst commentary

  • No significant alpha anywhere. None of the five CAPM windows, and none of the 20 factor-model regressions (CAPM on the FF market factor, FF3, Carhart and FF5, each over 36, 60, 120 and 180 months and the full sample), produces an intercept significant at 5% under Newey-West HAC inference. The smallest factor-model p-value is Carhart over the full sample (α = 7.09%/yr, p = 0.103).
  • Long-run point estimates are positive but imprecise. Over the full 415-month sample (Mar 1992 – Sep 2026) CAPM α is 4.50%/yr (×12) with a 95% HAC interval of roughly −4.5% to +13.5% per year. 120- and 180-month estimates are similar (6.5% and 5.4%/yr) with the same conclusion: the data cannot distinguish SNPS from zero alpha.
  • The most recent three years are sharply negative. The 36-month window (Oct 2023 – Sep 2026) shows α of −25.3%/yr (HAC t = −1.89, p = 0.068): borderline, not significant at 5%. Over that window SNPS compounded at −1.8%/yr against +22.9%/yr for the S&P 500 TR while carrying a beta of 1.44. The largest single month in the window is Sep 2025 (−18.2% vs +3.7% for the index).
  • The positive-alpha era is clustered. Every statistically significant positive rolling-60-month alpha ends between Apr 2019 and Jun 2024 (16% of 60-month windows); no 60-month window shows significant negative alpha. That period is now rolling out of the window, which is why the latest 60-month α is −3.3%/yr.
  • Beta is above one, but not decisively. Full-sample β = 1.19 (OLS test of β = 1: p = 0.054). Every window's 95% HAC beta interval includes 1. Rolling beta has moved through three regimes: about 1.5–2.9 before 2008, about 0.5–0.7 in 2010–14, and about 1.0–1.3 since 2017, with the 36-month figure rising to 1.44 most recently.
  • Style exposure: a persistent growth tilt. HML loadings are negative and highly significant in FF3 over 60–180 months (−0.69 to −0.72, HAC p ≤ 0.008). In FF5, part of that tilt moves into CMA (−0.61 at 180 months, p = 0.032), which is consistent with a high-investment, growth profile. Size, profitability (RMW) and momentum loadings are not significant in any window.
  • Idiosyncratic risk dominates. CAPM R² is only 0.25–0.37; residual volatility is 21–30% a year. At the full-sample α the implied information ratio is about 0.15, well below the level that would make a historical alpha estimate statistically robust over 34 years.

Formulas

CAPM:            (R_i,t − R_f,t) = α + β·(R_m,t − R_f,t) + ε_t,   OLS with intercept, t = 1…T
Returns:         R_t = P_t / P_(t−1) − 1, P = last available month-end total-return level (Adj Close / TR index)
β:               Cov(y,x) / Var(x)          α: ȳ − β·x̄          R² = ρ²(y,x) (single regressor)
SE (OLS):        σ̂²·(X'X)⁻¹, σ̂² = SSR/(T−2)
SE (HAC):        Newey-West, Bartlett kernel, lag L = ⌊4·(T/100)^(2/9)⌋, small-sample factor T/(T−k);
                 inference uses t(T−k). Lags used: 
                 36→3, 60→3, 120→4, 180→4, MAX(T=415)→5
Annualisation:   arithmetic α_ann = 12·α (primary).  (1+α)^12 − 1 is a mechanical compounding transform of the
                 intercept, NOT a realised investment return.
Jensen's α:      mean(R_i − R_f) − β·mean(R_m − R_f)  — algebraically identical to the OLS intercept on the same sample.
Geometric Jensen:(CAGR_i − CAGR_f) − β·(CAGR_m − CAGR_f); differs from 12·α by ≈ ½(σ²_i − β·σ²_m) (volatility drag) plus
                 compounding/cross terms.
Raw excess:      CAGR_i − CAGR_m (realised, geometric) and 12·mean(R_i − R_m) (arithmetic). Not risk-adjusted.
Residual vol:    √(SSR/(T−k))·√12.   Total vol: sd(R_i − R_f)·√12.
DW:              Σ(e_t − e_(t−1))² / Σe_t²  (≈2 ⇒ no first-order autocorrelation)
Breusch-Pagan:   LM = T·R² of e² on regressors, χ²(k−1)
Significance:    two-sided, 5%. Rolling-chart shading uses HAC p-values.

Assumptions

  • Benchmark for CAPM: SP500TR.csv (column Adj Close). Must be a total-return index.
  • Risk-free: Ken French RF (1-month T-bill return, Ibbotson/CRSP), monthly, from the FF3 file.
  • Provenance: Prices retrieved 2026-11-16 from the Yahoo Finance chart API (daily adjusted close; last trading day of each month used as the month-end level; SNPS pays no dividend, splits 2:1 in Sep-1995 and Sep-2003 are adjusted). Factors from Ken French Data Library files created using the 202609 CRSP database. Every transferred file was verified against checksums computed at source.
  • Factor models use Ken French factors, whose market factor (Mkt−RF, CRSP value-weighted all-US) differs from the S&P 500 TR; the "CAPM (FF Mkt−RF)" bridge row isolates that benchmark effect before adding SMB/HML/RMW/CMA/Mom.
  • Common sample: 1992-03 – 2026-09 (415 months). All CAPM windows end at 2026-09. Factor windows end at the last month available in every factor file used.
  • Incomplete months are excluded; missing months are never interpolated. As-of date: 2026-11-16.

9. Investment implications

Verdict. SNPS has not demonstrated statistically significant risk-adjusted outperformance in any window or model tested. Its long-run historical record is consistent with a modestly positive alpha (point estimates about 4–7.5% a year), but it is equally consistent with zero. The last three years run against it, with a borderline-significant negative CAPM alpha. Nothing here is a forecast.
  • Raw outperformance is mostly beta and growth exposure. Since March 1992, SNPS compounded at 12.4% a year against 10.9% for the S&P 500 TR, a raw gap of 1.5% a year. After adjusting for a beta of about 1.2, the realised (geometric) Jensen's alpha is about −0.2% a year. The positive arithmetic intercept of 4.5% a year is offset by volatility drag of about 4.7% a year, because SNPS's total volatility is about 35%.
  • Underwriting a position. Treat SNPS as a high-beta, growth-tilted holding with a large stock-specific risk component, not as a source of proven alpha. Size it off the 60-month beta of 1.22 and 29% residual volatility.
  • Factor hedging. The negative HML and CMA loadings mean SNPS tends to lag when value and conservative-investment factors rally. That exposure should be budgeted alongside other growth and technology holdings.
  • Historical vs. forward-looking. All alphas here are in-sample estimates of past average risk-adjusted returns. They are not forecasts. A significant historical intercept is consistent with skill, with a persistent unpriced risk exposure, or with a sample-specific re-rating (multiple expansion), and these cannot be separated by regression alone.
  • Model dependence. Compare CAPM α to the FF3/Carhart/FF5 intercepts: a large drop after adding factors means part of the "outperformance" is compensation for style exposures (e.g. negative HML/value tilt, positive RMW/profitability) rather than idiosyncratic return.
  • Beta for risk budgeting. Use the 60-month HAC beta and its confidence interval for position sizing; the 36-month estimate is noisier, and the rolling chart shows how unstable a single point estimate can be.
  • Residual (idiosyncratic) volatility is the relevant input for active-risk budgeting: an information ratio ≈ α_ann / residual vol.
  • Expected alpha going forward should be shrunk heavily toward zero; the width of the α confidence intervals is the honest measure of how little the history pins it down.
