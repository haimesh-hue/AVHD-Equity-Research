CoreWeave ER101
Equity Research 101 · First-principles overview

CoreWeave, Inc.

Bottom line
  • Business: the largest independent "neocloud", renting Nvidia GPU capacity to AI labs and hyperscalers on multi-year take-or-pay contracts. Revenue is compounding at over 100% a year with a $104B backlog.
  • Quality: not yet a good business on returns. Adjusted ROIC is under 3% while unsecured debt yields approach 15%. Every incremental dollar of growth is debt funded.
  • Valuation: cheap on forward sales (EV about 6x 2026E revenue), but the equity is a thin slice on top of ~$46B net debt, so small changes in exit multiple swing the share price violently.
  • Main risk: refinancing and counterparty risk. The story breaks if credit markets close before capex slows and cash flow turns positive.

01Business overview

CoreWeave builds and runs data centers full of Nvidia GPUs and rents that compute, mostly to train and serve large AI models. Customers sign multi-year contracts for reserved capacity (98% of revenue is committed), often prepaying part of it. CoreWeave uses those contracts as collateral to borrow, buys GPUs, leases powered data-center shells, and earns the spread between contract revenue and the cost of power, depreciation and interest.

How it makes money

  • Reserved GPU capacity on 2 to 6 year contracts, billed per GPU-hour (the bulk of revenue)
  • On-demand capacity at higher rates
  • Software layer: managed Kubernetes, Mission Control, Weights & Biases tooling
  • Customers: Microsoft (about 67% of 2025 revenue), OpenAI, Meta, Anthropic, plus a growing enterprise list

History

A crypto miner that pivoted at exactly the right moment. Its edge came from buying H100s early and having Nvidia as an investor and supplier. It is now a capital-intensive infrastructure lessor more than a software company.

02Moat

Real advantages

  • Speed: first to commercially deploy Nvidia GB300 NVL72 systems; stands up clusters faster than hyperscalers
  • Priority allocation from Nvidia, which owns about 10.7% of Class A shares and invested $2B in Jan 2026
  • Purpose-built AI stack and performance tuning that frontier labs value
  • Contracted power pipeline of 3.7 GW (4.2 GW incl. early Q3 adds); power is the scarce input

Why it may not last

  • The product (Nvidia GPUs in a building with power) is replicable by Oracle, Nebius, IREN and hyperscalers
  • Largest customers are also its competitors and are building their own capacity
  • Cost of capital disadvantage: hyperscalers fund near investment grade rates, CoreWeave at B+
  • No switching costs once a contract ends; capacity gets re-bid

A time-based edge (speed, allocation, power), not a structural one. It is real today because demand exceeds supply. Whether it survives a balanced market is the central question.

03Drivers

Revenue ≈ active power (MW) × utilization × revenue per MW. Contracted power converts into active power as sites are energized, and backlog converts into revenue as capacity goes live.

The single number to watch is active power: 1.5 GW at Q2 2026 against 3.7 GW contracted. Each delayed megawatt pushes revenue right while debt costs keep running. Revenue per MW is our estimate from Q2 annualized revenue over average active power.

04Financials

Profit and loss ($M)

RevenueOperating cash flowCapex

Revenue went from $16M (2022) to $7.6B (TTM). Adjusted EBITDA margins near 58% look superb, but depreciation and interest consume all of it: GAAP operating income is slightly negative and the TTM net loss is $1.9B. Interest alone is ~25% of revenue.

Balance sheet ($M)

Assets quadrupled in 18 months, funded by $51.6B of debt and leases against only $5.0B of equity. Working capital is deeply negative because customers prepay ($8.2B of deferred revenue at Dec-25), which is a genuine source of funding.

Cash flow ($M)

Operating cash flow is real and growing ($6.9B TTM), but capex is 3x larger. FCF conversion is not meaningful while both FCF and profit are negative. Capital allocation is single-minded: everything goes to capex, funded by debt, converts and equity (Nvidia, Jane Street). No dividends to common holders.

05Ratios

Leverage is 10.5x adjusted EBITDA including leases (6.7x excluding). Adjusted operating income covers interest only 0.2 to 0.5 times. Asset turnover of 0.15x means each dollar of assets earns 15 cents of revenue a year, so returns depend on high margins sustained over the full asset life. DuPont ROE is distorted by tiny book equity.

06Valuation

P/E and PEG are not meaningful (losses through at least 2027 on consensus). On EV/forward sales CoreWeave trades below Oracle and Nebius. Against its own short history, the stock is 57% off its 52-week high of $141.79, below the $87.20 Nvidia paid in Jan 2026, and above its $40 IPO price.

07Industry and competitors

AI compute demand is still growing faster than supply; management targets at least 8 GW of active power by 2030. Peers range from cash-rich hyperscalers to other leveraged neoclouds. Trailing metrics, close on Oct 19, 2026; EBITDA as reported by StockAnalysis.

CoreWeave is the cheapest neocloud on forward sales and the most levered. Oracle is the closest scaled comparison and also burning cash on AI capex, but it has a profitable legacy business underneath. Microsoft shows what a moated cloud earns: 26% ROIC.

08Management

Who runs it

  • Michael Intrator, co-founder and CEO since 2017 (9 years); 38.7% of voting power
  • Brian Venturo, co-founder, Chief Strategy Officer; 20.3% of votes
  • Brannin McBee, co-founder, Chief Development Officer; 14.6% of votes, 10b5-1 sale plan from Mar 2026
  • Nitin Agrawal, CFO since 2024 (ex-Google); Sachin Jain, COO (ex-Oracle)

Governance and allocation

  • Dual class: Class B carries 10 votes; insiders control ~72% of votes until a 2032 sunset
  • Plan: 2026 capex $35 to $39B, at least 8 GW active by 2030
  • Bolt-on software M&A: Weights & Biases, OpenPipe, Monolith AI; the $9B Core Scientific deal was voted down by CORZ holders in Oct 2025
  • Financing creativity: delayed-draw term loans secured by contracts, converts, a Eurobond

Founder-led with heavy ownership, which aligns incentives on upside but leaves outside shareholders no real vote. Management has consistently chosen growth over balance-sheet safety.

09Risks

Financial

  • Refinancing: S&P rating B+; unsecured yields near 15% in Oct 2026; stock fell 20% in the week to Oct 16 on debt fears
  • Interest burden: $640M in Q2 alone, about 25% of revenue
  • Dilution: equity raises and converts as capex outruns cash flow

Business

  • Concentration: top 3 customers ~72% of Q2 revenue; top one ~36%
  • Counterparty: backlog is only as good as customers' ability to pay, including cash-burning AI labs
  • Obsolescence: GPU depreciation lives may prove too long if Nvidia's cadence makes older chips uneconomic
  • Execution and politics: power and construction delays; state moratoria on data centers

These risks compound each other. A delay pushes revenue out, which weakens coverage, which raises the cost of the next financing.

11Takeaways

Remember

  • Hypergrowth with real contracted demand ($104B backlog plus $25B added early Q3)
  • Economics today: ~58% adj. EBITDA margin, ~0% GAAP operating margin, ~25% of revenue to interest
  • Returns on capital far below cost of capital; value depends entirely on the post-buildout steady state
  • Highly levered equity: outcome range is very wide

Check next (Q3 report, ~Nov 11, 2026)

  • Active power vs 1.5 GW, and any slippage in energization
  • 2027 capex framework and how it will be funded
  • Terms of the next debt raise (spread, secured vs unsecured)
  • Microsoft share of revenue and new investment-grade customers
  • Depreciation life assumptions and any impairments
Glossary and definitions
Neocloud
A cloud provider focused on renting GPUs for AI, as opposed to a general-purpose hyperscaler.
Backlog / RPO
Contracted revenue not yet recognized. $104.2B at Jun-26, of which $103.7B is remaining performance obligations.
Active vs contracted power
Megawatts energized and earning revenue vs megawatts secured under lease but not yet live.
Adjusted EBITDA
Company-defined: excludes stock compensation and other items. Used here because GAAP EBITDA is not reported.
Net debt
Debt plus finance and operating lease liabilities, minus unrestricted cash. Peers measured the same way (StockAnalysis basis).
ROIC (adj.)
Adjusted operating income × (1 − 21% tax) ÷ average (equity + debt incl. leases − cash).
DuPont ROE
Net margin × asset turnover × equity multiplier, using average assets and average book equity.
TTM
Trailing twelve months to Jun 30, 2026 = FY25 − H1'25 + H1'26.
