1. What it does and how it makes money

Anthropic, PBC builds the Claude family of AI models and sells access to them. It is a public benefit corporation, founded in January 2021 by seven former OpenAI researchers led by Dario and Daniela Amodei.

Two revenue streams. Consumption: businesses and developers pay per token (unit of text processed) through the API and through cloud marketplaces. Subscriptions: monthly Claude plans for individuals and teams, plus Claude Code, its agentic coding product.

In the first nine months of 2026, consumption revenue grew by $24.2bn and subscriptions by $6.5bn, so roughly four fifths of the growth came from usage-based selling to businesses.

How it got here

    2. Moat: why it earns its returns, and can rivals copy it?

    Real advantages

    • Coding and agent leadership. Menlo Ventures put Claude at 54% of enterprise coding spend vs 21% for OpenAI (Dec 2025), and estimates Claude Code plus OpenAI's Codex take 70% of a $34bn AI coding market (Nov 2026).
    • Enterprise trust. Anthropic's share of enterprise LLM API spend rose from 12% in 2023 to 40% (Menlo, Dec 2025). Over 1,900 customers pay $1m+ a year; about 250 pay $10m+.
    • Workflow lock-in. Net revenue retention of about 900%: customers who were paying a year ago now pay about 9x more. Agents built around one model family are costly to re-test and re-tune.
    • Capital and compute access. $141bn raised before the IPO, plus a $26bn strategic placement from Google, NVIDIA, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC.

    What is copyable

    • Model leads decay fast. Frontier capability gaps have historically closed within months; OpenAI, Google and open-weight models compete on every release.
    • Low switching cost at the API level. Swapping a model endpoint is cheap; the lock-in sits in tooling, evals and agents, not contracts.
    • Price. Analysts cited by The Next Web found businesses hitting AI budget limits and some moving to cheaper models.

    3. Drivers: what revenue is made of

    Revenue = tokens used x price per token x model mix + seats x subscription price. Volume is the engine: every new model release (Claude 4 in May 2025 onward) has lifted usage per customer. Price per token tends to fall each generation, so growth depends on usage rising faster than price falls.

    8. Risks that would break the story

    1. Compute commitments outrun revenue. About $70.7bn falls due in the next 12 months and $451.6bn before 2031, against an $81bn run-rate. If growth stalls, these are fixed costs. Q3 compute cost grew 382% YoY and ate 58% of revenue, up from 48% in Q2.
    2. Growth deceleration is already visible. QoQ growth fell from 144% in Q2 to 47% in Q3. Run-rate grew about 4-5% a month into October by one forecaster's reading.
    3. Price war and model commoditisation. Cheaper models from OpenAI, Google and open-weight labs compress price per token.
    4. Government conflict. The Defense Department labelled Anthropic a supply-chain risk in Feb 2026 after it refused autonomous-weapons and mass-surveillance uses; litigation is ongoing.
    5. Dilution and lock-ups. 1.77bn options and 1.13bn RSUs sit on 13.2bn shares; about $14.2bn of RSU expense is recognised on the IPO. Some 3.7bn shares can be sold before the 180-day lock-up ends.
    6. Governance. Public holders cannot replace the board; the mission can legally outrank returns in a PBC.
    7. Customer concentration by size. Customers paying $100k+ make up 74% of revenue; a few large API and cloud channels matter a lot.

        Method, glossary and sources

        Metric definitions
          Glossary
          • Run-rate revenue: Anthropic's definition, last 28 days of consumption fees x 13 plus current monthly subscriptions x 12.
          • Net revenue retention (NRR): this year's revenue from last year's customers divided by their revenue a year ago.
          • Adjusted operating income: GAAP operating income plus stock-based compensation, non-cash charitable contributions and the $1.5bn Bartz copyright settlement.
          • EV/Sales: enterprise value (market cap minus net cash) divided by revenue; used here because earnings are not yet meaningful.
          • PBC: public benefit corporation; directors must balance shareholder returns with a stated public benefit.
